PicoExpense vs Monarch Money

Monarch Money is a leading paid successor to all-in-one apps like Mint: aggregation, net worth, and shared household views. PicoExpense does not try to be a complete financial OS.

Feature PicoExpense Monarch Money
Cost Free — no app subscription Paid after a short trial. Core is commonly listed around $99.99/year; a higher Plus plan exists. No permanent free tier.
What it is Personal expense tracker: accounts, categories, transactions, budgets, goals. Household finance dashboard: cash flow, budgets, goals, recurring bills, investments, net worth.
Where data lives This device. You download JSON (or encrypt it, or copy a Drive zip). Monarch’s cloud. Bank connections go through aggregators (e.g. Plaid / MX / Finicity).
Bank & broker sync No. CSV import only. Yes — this is the point of the product.
Investments Not in this version (on purpose). Yes — holdings roll into net worth.
Household sharing No live multi-user. Built for couples and households with invited members.
Account required No Yes
Offline Works without the internet after load. A connected web/app service.
Privacy posture No PicoExpense login; no telemetry in the app. Vendor account plus linked institutions. Fine if you want aggregation; a different trust model.

Which should you choose?

Choose PicoExpense when…

  • You only need expenses, income, transfers, and simple budgets
  • You will not connect every bank and brokerage to a dashboard
  • You want a free tool that still has CSV import, backups, and local OCR
  • Investments and net worth can live somewhere else (or not at all)

Choose Monarch when…

  • You want one paid place for cash, credit, and investments
  • Two people need the same live household picture
  • Automatic categorisation from bank feeds is worth ~$100/year
  • You are replacing Mint-style aggregation, not a paper notebook
